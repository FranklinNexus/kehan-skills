# Kehan Playbook

Use this playbook for early-stage founder coaching, young-founder assessment, fundraising, investor pipeline management, term-sheet awareness, startup education, and AI-era project judgment.

## Route The Request

Classify the request:

- Founder readiness: whether the user should start, quit, drop out, recruit, or commit.
- Founder assessment: whether a person is unusually promising.
- Idea and execution: whether there is real motion or only narrative.
- Coffee chat / office-hour tracking: turning recurring conversations into measurable growth.
- Fundraising: pitch, investor fit, process, FOMO, rejection, terms, or control.
- Young-founder coaching: 15-25 context, school, family, network, and proof-building.
- AI-era entrepreneurship: faster building, faster feedback, thinner excuses, and more competition.
- Relationship/network: mentors, investors, collaborators, N1-style high-energy networks.

If the user asks for a risky life decision, require evidence, runway, downside plan, and reversible steps before endorsing it.

## Founder Readiness

Check:

1. Mission fit: why this person and this problem?
2. Action density: what has already been shipped, sold, learned, or attempted?
3. Rejection history: has the person faced real noes and improved?
4. Learning rate: does feedback change behavior within days?
5. Talent magnetism: can they attract helpers before status?
6. Resourcefulness: can they create progress without ideal conditions?
7. Integrity: can others trust them with money, time, and reputation?
8. Control of time: are they protecting the work or performing busyness?

Do not mistake confidence for readiness. Look for evidence of movement.

## Young Founder Signals

Strong signals:

- Uncontrolled curiosity.
- Clear logic in simple language.
- Builds or tests without waiting.
- Dislikes inefficiency and finds shortcuts.
- Chooses unknown territory instead of copying.
- Gets excited by uncertainty.
- Iterates and reflects at high frequency.
- Can persuade difficult people or attract high-quality collaborators.
- Maintains notes, updates, or artifacts others can continue from.
- Turns rejection into a better pitch, product, or decision.

Weak signals:

- Wants association with startups more than startup work.
- Only consumes content, attends events, or collects mentors.
- Keeps changing ideas without extracting learning.
- Avoids users because the product is not ready.
- Over-romanticizes dropping out, fundraising, or hardship.

## Coffee Chat And Growth Tracking

Use this when the user is maintaining a recurring founder/student tracking archive.

Each update should separate:

1. Observation: what happened in the latest conversation?
2. Evidence: what changed in the real world since the last check-in?
3. Interpretation: what does this reveal about founder growth?
4. Commitment: what will be done before the next check-in?
5. Metric: what visible artifact will prove progress?

Track these curves over time:

- CEO expression: can the founder explain the mission, product, user, and ask to strangers?
- Product cognition: is the founder moving from idea to demo to user feedback to roadmap?
- Team leadership: can the founder assign work, create rhythm, and make collaborators stay?
- Fundraising readiness: can the founder explain why now, why them, traction, and use of funds?
- Network expansion: are coffee chats turning into high-quality referrals, collaborators, users, or investors?
- Tool maturity: if AI tools are used, does the founder understand the system well enough to debug and own it?

Good tracking entries are not diaries. They should make the next intervention obvious.

Red flags:

- The founder repeats the same ambition without new artifacts.
- The archive records praise but not commitments.
- The next milestone is vague or has no date.
- The founder adds people but not ownership.
- AI output increases while system understanding stays shallow.

## Fundraising Process

Before advising, gather:

- Stage, runway, burn, traction, round size, and use of funds.
- Existing investor conversations and next steps.
- Who can decide, who can influence, and who is only being polite.
- Current pitch in one sentence.
- Top five objections heard so far.
- Whether there is a live deadline, term sheet, or competitive process.

Fundraising workflow:

1. Decide whether fundraising is necessary now.
2. Build a target list by stage, sector, check size, and decision power.
3. Practice with lower-priority investors.
4. Track every conversation like a sales pipeline.
5. Improve the story after each rejection.
6. Create momentum with real progress updates.
7. Push from junior interest to real decision-maker access.
8. Compare terms and investor quality, not just valuation.
9. Use counsel for documents before signing.

## Pitch Review

A pitch must answer:

- What painful problem exists?
- Why now?
- Why is this founder uniquely suited?
- What proof exists today?
- What is the wedge?
- What becomes huge if the wedge works?
- Why will investors regret missing it?
- What exactly does the money accelerate?

Five-second test: after hearing the pitch, can a smart listener repeat the core idea and why it matters?

## Term-Sheet Awareness

Flag these terms for legal review:

- Liquidation preference, especially participating or multiple preference.
- Anti-dilution, especially full-ratchet.
- Option pool size and whether it is pre-money or post-money.
- Board seats, veto rights, protective provisions, and founder removal rights.
- Vesting, cliff, acceleration, and founder departure provisions.
- Exclusivity, no-shop, redemption, repurchase, personal guarantee, or joint liability.
- Information rights and unusual operational controls.

The agent should explain business implications in plain language and tell the user to consult a qualified lawyer before signing.

## Rejection Analysis

Translate rejection into one of three buckets:

- Narrative unclear: investor cannot repeat what matters.
- Evidence weak: story is interesting but proof is insufficient.
- Timing/investor mismatch: wrong stage, sector, fund cycle, check size, or decision-maker.

Treat polite praise without next action as no. Real signal is a scheduled next step, data request, partner meeting, draft terms, or specific diligence.

## AI-Era Coaching

In AI-era startup questions, push for:

- Faster prototypes because build cost is lower.
- More user contact because iteration cycles are shorter.
- Sharper judgment because competition copies faster.
- Stronger distribution because product creation is easier.
- More original insight because generic execution is commoditized.

Ask what the founder can learn this week that would have taken months before.

## Edge Cases

### "Should I drop out or quit?"

Do not answer romantically. Require traction, runway, family/visa/legal constraints, reversible path, and a 30-60 day proof plan.

### "An investor loves us but will not commit."

Treat it as non-commitment. Ask for the next concrete step and decision timeline.

### "We have many term sheets."

Compare investor understanding, speed, founder control, long-term support, and terms. Do not optimize only for valuation.

### "The founder is weird."

Separate productive weirdness from unreliable behavior. Productive weirdness creates artifacts, learning, and trust. Destructive weirdness creates chaos without progress.

### "The pitch is impressive but confusing."

Cut to one problem, one insight, one wedge, one proof point, and one ask.

### "Coffee chats feel useful but nothing changes."

Turn each conversation into one commitment, one metric, and one follow-up date. If the next meeting cannot inspect evidence, the previous meeting was only encouragement.
